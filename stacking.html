    Battle Calculator Guide

    How Epic Battles Are Calculated

    Follow the complete calculation, or open only the section you need.

    1Battle overview

    An Epic battle is a sequence of attacks between your surviving squads and the Epic’s surviving enemy squads. The calculator models each friendly squad separately, including its health, target matchups, attack opportunities, and expected damage.

    Opening initiative

    The first attack is a 50/50 coin toss: either your army or the Epic attacks first. After that opening cycle, the Epic attacks first in every later cycle. The calculator simulates both opening outcomes and averages them.

    A normal Epic formation contains one Flying, Mounted, Melee, and Ranged enemy squad. Arachne contains two of each. Custom encounters may contain from one to eight enemy squads.

    2Unit bonus assignment

    Every unit uses one bonus profile. Species takes priority over troop class: Beasts, Dragons, Elementals, and Giants use their matching species profile even when the unit appears in a Specialist or mercenary group.

    Monster speciesBeast · Dragon · Elemental · Giant
    Human classesGuardsman · Specialist · Engineer
    Special profilesEpic Hunter

    Cursed, Demon, Elves, Undead, and Barbarian units use Guardsman bonuses. Human mercenaries without a Guardsman, Specialist, or Engineer class also use Guardsman bonuses. Mercenaries otherwise follow the same species rules as every other unit.

    Rows marked Auto follow their parent Monsters or Humans row. Turning Auto off lets that profile use its own Double Damage, Strike Twice, Health, and Strength values.

    3Epic battle damage

    Base Strength is the unit’s original strength. Profile Strength is the Strength value from the unit’s assigned Beast, Guardsman, Specialist, or other profile. Strength PvE is a global bonus applied to every unit fighting an Epic.

    Strength per unitBase Strength × (1 + Profile Strength + Strength PvE + Epic bonus + matchup bonus + Arachne bonus)

    An Epic bonus belongs to a specific unit and applies against Epic monsters. A matchup bonus applies only when that unit attacks a matching Flying, Mounted, Melee, or Ranged enemy squad. An intrinsic Arachne bonus applies only in an Arachne encounter.

    Example

    A unit has 1,000 Base Strength, 2,000% Profile Strength, 3,000% Strength PvE, a 100% Epic bonus, and a 50% bonus against its target.

    1,000 × (1 + 20 + 30 + 1 + 0.5) = 52,500 damage

    The extra 1 represents the unit’s original 100% Base Strength.

    4Double Damage and Strike Twice

    Double Damage is the chance that an attack deals twice its normal damage. The calculator adds the unit’s intrinsic Double Damage to its profile value and caps the final chance at 100%. For example, a Smiter with an intrinsic 5% bonus and a 14% profile value has a 19% Double Damage chance.

    Strike Twice is the chance to attack a second, distinct enemy squad. Damage is calculated separately for that second target, so the first target’s matchup bonus is not automatically reused.

    Expected damage per opportunity(1 + Double Damage chance) × (first-strike damage + Strike Twice chance × second-strike damage)
    Four-squad example

    A ranged Guardsman deals 150 damage to Flying because of a matchup bonus, but 100 damage to its next-best target. With 20% Strike Twice:

    150 + (0.20 × 100) = 170 expected damage

    If it also receives the same matchup bonus against Melee, its second strike may deal 150 damage instead. With Arachne, two copies of each enemy type exist, so both strikes can sometimes receive the same matchup bonus while still targeting different squads.

    5Health and predicted death order
    Effective squad healthQuantity × Base Health × (1 + Profile Health)

    The calculator uses effective squad health to construct the predicted death ladder. Higher-health friendly squads are expected to be defeated earlier; lower-health squads remain for later attack opportunities.

    Minimum Separation uses the smallest legal health difference that preserves the requested order. Fixed separation requires a larger percentage gap between adjacent squads.

    Why capacity can finish slightly below 100%

    Quantities must be whole legal increments, remain below the capacity limit, and preserve the health ladder. A final deterministic top-up adds every legal increment it can. Any remaining space may be too small for another unit or adding one may reverse the intended death order.

    6Calculation methods
    Custom

    Builds legal quantities and an automatic death order from the selected units. The player can then change the order, and the calculator preserves those changes.

    Optimize

    Simulates many quantity and death-order structures and keeps the army with the strongest Expected Lifetime Damage.

    7Optimizer workflow

    The optimizer searches only the units selected by the player. When mercenaries are excluded, their quantities remain fixed and their damage is included in the final score.

    1. Validate and prepareResolve unit profiles, bonuses, capacity limits, enemy formation, and legal quantity rules.
    2. Screen independent seedsCreate several different starting army structures so the search is not dependent on one initial guess.
    3. Local refinementTransfer progressively smaller amounts of capacity between squads and keep improvements.
    4. Structural explorationTest alternate death orders, attack-opportunity thresholds, counterfactual battle basins, and related squad-group redistributions.
    5. Precision polishRevisit the strongest candidates with smaller changes until no tested move improves ELD.
    6. Practical tie-breakWhen armies are nearly equal, prefer a more practical death order within the allowed near-optimal range.
    7. Canonical final scoreRe-run the selected army through the shared battle simulator and display its quantities, death order, ELD, and revival cost.
    8Reading the results
    Expected Lifetime Damage (ELD)

    The average total damage the army is expected to cause across its predicted attack opportunities. It is a comparison metric, not a guarantee for one battle.

    Gold Revival

    The Temple-adjusted Gold cost for 90% revivable attacking losses. Whole-unit rounding is applied.

    Damage per 1,000 Gold

    Expected Lifetime Damage divided by revival cost, expressed per 1,000 Gold.

    Unusual death orders

    A squad may die earlier because sacrificing one attack can allow several stronger squads to gain attacks. The optimizer compares total army ELD rather than judging one squad in isolation.
